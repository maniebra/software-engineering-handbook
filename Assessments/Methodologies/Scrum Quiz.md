# Scrum Quiz

16 questions covering [Scrum](../../Methodologies/Scrum/index.md): roles, events,
artifacts, [Sprints](../../Methodologies/Scrum/Sprints.md) and the
[Definition of Done](../../Methodologies/Scrum/Definition%20of%20Done.md).

## Roles

<quiz>
The Product Owner is on holiday for a Sprint and nobody has been delegated authority. What is the main risk?

- [ ] The Sprint cannot start without a Sprint Planning attendee
- [ ] The Scrum Master must write the Sprint Goal alone
- [x] Priority questions stall or get answered by the team, so business value decisions drift to people not accountable for them
> Correct. The Product Owner role is a single accountable decision-maker precisely so that priority never becomes a committee outcome.
- [ ] Velocity will be inaccurate for that Sprint
</quiz>

<quiz>
Which action is inconsistent with the Scrum Master role?

- [ ] Facilitating the Retrospective
- [x] Assigning Sprint Backlog items to individual developers
> Correct. Developers pull their own work. Assignment removes self-organization, which is where most of the team's adaptive capacity lives.
- [ ] Removing an impediment caused by another department
- [ ] Coaching the Product Owner on backlog refinement
</quiz>

<quiz>
Scrum states there are no sub-teams within the Developers. What does that rule out?

- [x] A separate testing group that owns quality while others own coding
> Correct. The whole team is accountable for the Definition of Done, and a handoff to a testing sub-team reintroduces a phase.
- [ ] Developers with different levels of seniority
- [ ] Specialists in security or performance
- [ ] Pairing between two developers on one item
</quiz>

<quiz>
Who is accountable for maximizing the value of the product?

- [ ] The Scrum Master
- [ ] The Developers collectively
- [ ] The Business Sponsor
- [x] The Product Owner
> Correct. Which is why they alone order the Product Backlog and may refuse work.
</quiz>

## Events

<quiz>
Sprint Planning answers three questions. Which is the first?

- [ ] How will the work be broken into tasks
- [ ] What can be Done within the Sprint
- [x] Why is this Sprint valuable, which produces the Sprint Goal
> Correct. Value comes before selection and decomposition, otherwise the Sprint is just a batch of tickets.
- [ ] Who will work on which item
</quiz>

<quiz>
The Daily Scrum has degenerated into each developer reporting status to the Scrum Master. What has been lost?

- [ ] The timebox, which is now longer than 15 minutes
- [x] Its purpose as a re-planning event for the Developers against the Sprint Goal
> Correct. It is a planning event for the people doing the work. Reporting upward makes it overhead rather than coordination.
- [ ] The Product Owner's visibility into progress
- [ ] The opportunity to update the burndown chart
</quiz>

<quiz>
A team prepares slides describing what was built for the Sprint Review. What does that signal?

- [x] That a Done Increment probably does not exist, since a real Increment can simply be shown working
> Correct. The Review inspects the Increment, and a description is not an Increment.
- [ ] Good preparation, since stakeholders need context
- [ ] That the Review timebox is too short
- [ ] That the Product Owner has not refined the backlog
</quiz>

<quiz>
What is the correct output of a Sprint Retrospective?

- [ ] A list of every problem raised, sent to management
- [ ] A revised Sprint Goal for the Sprint just completed
- [ ] An updated Definition of Ready
- [x] One or two improvements pulled into the next Sprint Backlog, so they compete for real capacity
> Correct. Improvements that are not given capacity are how retrospectives lose credibility over a few months.
</quiz>

<quiz>
Backlog refinement is best described as what?

- [ ] A formal Scrum event with its own timebox
- [ ] A meeting run by the Scrum Master before each Sprint
- [x] An ongoing activity, commonly capped near 10% of capacity, that keeps upcoming items ready enough for Planning
> Correct. It is not one of the five events, but skipping it turns Sprint Planning into a discovery session.
- [ ] The process of splitting the Sprint Backlog into tasks
</quiz>

## Artifacts

<quiz>
Each Scrum artifact carries a commitment. Which pairing is correct?

- [ ] Product Backlog and Definition of Done
- [x] Sprint Backlog and Sprint Goal
> Correct. Product Backlog carries the Product Goal, and the Increment carries the Definition of Done.
- [ ] Increment and Sprint Goal
- [ ] Sprint Backlog and Product Goal
</quiz>

<quiz>
Why does the Product Backlog have a "iceberg" shape, detailed at the top and vague further down?

- [x] Because refining items that may never be built, or that will change before they are, is waste
> Correct. Detail is added just in time, as items approach the top of the order.
- [ ] Because the Product Owner has limited time to write items
- [ ] Because low-priority items are never implemented
- [ ] Because Scrum forbids estimating items more than one Sprint ahead
</quiz>

<quiz>
Stakeholders treat the Sprint Backlog as a delivery commitment they can hold the team to. What is wrong?

- [ ] Nothing, the Sprint Backlog is a contract for the Sprint
- [ ] It should be a commitment only if the team estimated in hours
- [ ] Only the Scrum Master may show it to stakeholders
- [x] It is the Developers' own working plan, expected to change daily, and only the Sprint Goal is the commitment
> Correct. Scope inside a Sprint is renegotiated with the Product Owner as the team learns, which is why the Goal is stated as an outcome.
</quiz>

## Sprints and Done

<quiz>
Two days before a Sprint ends, it is clear two items will not be finished. What happens?

- [ ] The Sprint is extended by two days
- [ ] The items are marked Done and finished next Sprint
- [x] The Sprint ends on its fixed date, and the unfinished items return to the Product Backlog for re-ordering
> Correct. The timebox never moves, because a variable Sprint length destroys the cadence that makes forecasting possible.
- [ ] The Scrum Master cancels the Sprint
</quiz>

<quiz>
Who may cancel a Sprint, and when?

- [ ] The Scrum Master, when the team is blocked
- [x] The Product Owner, and only when the Sprint Goal has become obsolete
> Correct. It is rare, and "we will not finish everything" is never a reason, since unfinished work simply returns to the backlog.
- [ ] The Developers, by majority agreement
- [ ] Any stakeholder whose priorities changed
</quiz>

<quiz>
A team's Definition of Done omits deployment to staging. What is the predictable consequence?

- [x] Deployment work accumulates as undone work until a hardening phase is needed to absorb it
> Correct. Excluded work is deferred rather than cancelled, and it returns at a worse exchange rate.
- [ ] Velocity becomes more stable
- [ ] The Product Owner gains flexibility over releases
- [ ] Nothing, since deployment is an operations concern
</quiz>

<quiz>
What is the difference between a Definition of Done and a Definition of Ready?

- [ ] They are the same checklist applied at different times
- [ ] Ready is mandatory in Scrum, Done is optional
- [ ] Done applies to the Increment, Ready applies to the release
- [x] Done says how finished any item must be to count, while Ready says how prepared an item should be to enter a Sprint, and a strict Ready gate quietly recreates a requirements phase
> Correct. Ready is a useful guideline and a harmful gate, which is why the Scrum Guide names only the Definition of Done.
</quiz>
