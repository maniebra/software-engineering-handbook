# Scrum Principles

The working ideas behind the [Scrum](index.md) framework.

## The classic summary

- Development work is partitioned into "packets", meaning small, independently valuable
  backlog items rather than phases.
- Testing and documentation are on-going as the product is constructed, not a phase
  bolted on at the end.
- Work occurs in **[Sprints](Sprints.md)** and is derived from a **backlog** of
  existing requirements.
- Meetings are very short and, traditionally, sometimes conducted standing up, and the
  discomfort is the timebox.
- Demos are delivered to the customer within the time-box allocated.

## The pillars

Scrum is an *empirical* process: it assumes you cannot know the plan up front, so you
inspect reality frequently and adapt.

| Pillar | Meaning | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| **Transparency** | The work and its state are visible to everyone involved | Backlogs, Definition of Done |
| **Inspection** | Progress and artifacts are examined frequently | Daily Scrum, Sprint Review |
| **Adaptation** | What inspection reveals changes what happens next | Backlog re-ordering, Retrospective |

Removing any one pillar breaks the other two: you cannot inspect what is not
transparent, and inspection that changes nothing is theatre.

## The five values

**Commitment**, **Focus**, **Openness**, **Respect**, **Courage**. These are what make
the events useful rather than ritual. A Retrospective without openness produces
nothing, and a Daily Scrum without courage hides blockers until it is too late.

## Empirical vs. predictive

| | Predictive (plan-driven) | Empirical (Scrum) |
|---|---|---|
| Assumption | Requirements can be known up front | Requirements are discovered by building |
| Control | Conformance to the plan | Frequent inspection of a real Increment |
| Response to change | Change request, re-baseline | Re-order the Product Backlog next Sprint |
| Risk surfaces | At integration or acceptance | Every Sprint |

## Check Your Understanding

<quiz>
Scrum is called an "empirical" process. What does that mean in practice?

- [x] Decisions are based on what a real, inspected Increment reveals, rather than on a plan made before the work started
> Correct. Transparency, inspection and adaptation are the mechanism, and the Increment is the evidence.
- [ ] The team collects metrics on developer productivity
- [ ] Every requirement must be proven by a written specification
- [ ] Estimates are replaced by measured actuals from previous projects
</quiz>

<quiz>
A team runs Retrospectives but never changes anything as a result. Which pillar is broken?

- [ ] Transparency
- [x] Adaptation, because inspection that changes nothing is theatre
> Correct. All three pillars are load-bearing, and inspecting without adapting wastes the feedback the Sprint bought.
- [ ] Inspection
- [ ] Commitment
</quiz>
