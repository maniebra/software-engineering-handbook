# Agile

**Agile** is not a process. It is a set of values and principles, published in 2001 as
the *Manifesto for Agile Software Development*, that concrete methodologies such as
[Scrum](../Scrum/index.md), [XP](../Extreme%20Programming/index.md) and
[DSDM](../DSDM/index.md) implement in their own way.

## The four values

> **Individuals and interactions** over processes and tools
> **Working software** over comprehensive documentation
> **Customer collaboration** over contract negotiation
> **Responding to change** over following a plan
>
> That is, while there is value in the items on the right, we value the items on the left more.

The last line is the one teams forget. Agile does not say "no documentation" or
"no plans". It says that when the two sides conflict, the left side wins.

## Common misreadings

| Claim | Reality |
|---|---|
| "Agile means no documentation" | It means documentation that is *worth its maintenance cost*. |
| "Agile means no estimates or plans" | Plans exist, but are short-lived and revised on feedback. |
| "We do daily stand-ups, so we are agile" | Ceremony without short feedback loops and shippable increments is not agility. |
| "Agile means the customer can change anything, anytime" | Change is welcomed *between* increments, not mid-Sprint. |
